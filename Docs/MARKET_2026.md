# The market Pantheon is shipping into — September 2026

**Bottom line.** The collector-RPG genre is bigger than ever but flat-to-shrinking on
mobile (the top 100 gacha games took an estimated **$616M in August 2026**, and mid-core
mobile spend fell about 7% in H1 2026), and the games that hold players now do it with
*systems that manufacture their own content* — rotating endgame gauntlets, roguelike
runs, and boss damage-checks that force you to use your whole box — not with more
stages. Pantheon already has more content surface than most launched games in this
genre; what it is missing is the **rotation** that makes 79 families matter and the
**sweep-and-determinism layer** that stops a rune game from feeling like an insult.
Almost everything worth stealing in 2026 works offline, single-player, with no server —
the server-shaped things (guilds, real PvP, live events) are the *least* important part
of what keeps modern players, and the legal ground under paid loot boxes is moving fast
enough that shipping with no real-money gacha at all is now a genuine competitive
position, not a limitation.

# The ten things worth stealing, ranked

Ranked by **retention gained per week of your work**, not by how impressive they are.
Bucket **A** = works fully offline with no backend. **B** = needs a server. **C** = do
not copy.

---

### 1. A rotating endgame gauntlet that grades you on *roster breadth*
**Bucket A · about 2–3 weeks**

**What it is.** Honkai: Star Rail runs three permanent endgame modes side by side —
**Memory of Chaos** (kill elites inside a cycle limit), **Pure Fiction** (kill as many
adds as possible in four cycles, scored on points) and **Apocalyptic Shadow** (kill a
boss in as few actions as possible) — and they **rotate every six weeks** with new
enemies and new run-wide buffs. Each half of each mode needs a *separate team*, so a
full clear costs you six built units, and each mode rewards a different team shape.
Genshin's **Imaginarium Theater** is the same idea taken further: it now demands a pool
of **28–32 eligible characters** and, in the words of one write-up, "quietly punishes
anyone who has poured everything into a single hyper-invested comp." [live]

**Why it fits Pantheon.** You have **79 families across five elements** and nothing in
the game that makes a player build more than one team. This is the single highest-value
thing on the list because the content is generated by a *rule*, not by art: an element
lock, a pantheon lock, a "no unit twice" lock, a run-wide buff, a turn cap. Six weeks of
new "content" costs you a row in a table. It is also the only item here that makes the
back half of your roster worth summoning for.

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. Add one new place on the island: three gauntlets, twelve floors each.
2. Give each gauntlet a different win condition — *survive N waves*, *score by damage in
   N turns*, *kill one boss in as few turns as possible*.
3. Each gauntlet is **two halves with no shared units**, so a full clear costs six to ten
   built families.
4. Add a `season` integer derived from the device clock (`weeks since a fixed epoch / 6`)
   and use it to index a table of: enemy set, element lock, and one run-wide buff.
5. Write twelve seasons into the table at launch. That is eighteen months of rotation
   before you ever touch it again.

### 2. Sweep — clear a stage you have already beaten, instantly, with no battle
**Bucket A · about 3 days**

**What it is.** Blue Archive lets you sweep cleared content instantly and collect the
rewards without replaying it; a 2026 round-up describes modern gacha as having "short
dailies, generous sweep and auto features, and no punishment for skipping a day." [live]
Summoners War itself shipped this in its **TOMORROW** update (November 2025) as **Scout
Battle**, which farms Cairos and Rift dungeons for **up to eight hours while you are
offline**, alongside **Ameria's Luck**, a daily 5× boost to high-grade drop rates. [live]

**Why it fits Pantheon.** Your auto-repeat (1/5/10/20 runs) still *plays the battles*.
That is 2014's answer. The 2026 answer is: you already proved you can clear it, here are
the drops, take ten seconds. Every farming loop in the game — halls, dungeons, campaign
re-runs — gets shorter, and the relic grind stops being the reason people quit.

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. Add `sweepCost` to a stage: energy × N, same as a real run.
2. On sweep, run `BattleEngine` headless with no scene, no animation, no HUD — or, if
   the outcome is already a foregone conclusion at that power ratio, skip the sim
   entirely and roll `StageRewards` N times.
3. Gate it on **three stars already earned** on that stage at that tier. That keeps the
   first clear meaningful and makes the star rating worth chasing.
4. Show the result as a single `SpoilsPanel` with the totals.

### 3. Deterministic relic targeting — let the player *aim* at a sub stat
**Bucket A · about 1 week**

**What it is.** Summoners War's **Reappraisal Stones** reroll a Legendary rune's four sub
stats while keeping slot, set, main stat and grade, and each roll **offers a choice
between two options**. Its November 2025 **Refinement Stones** and **Core Fragments**
"let players power up their Runes and Artifacts **while keeping desired sub
properties**." Epic Seven's **Steel Workshop** crafts gear to a chosen set and slot, and
its **reforge** raises +15 gear from level 85 to 90 with sub stats that "are guaranteed
to increase with a set value." [live]

**Why it fits Pantheon.** Your relic power-up is currently pure RNG with *failure* —
drachma burned for nothing, a 40% chance at +15. That is the exact mechanic that made
rune farming the most-complained-about system in the genre for a decade, and the genre
leader has now spent a flagship update walking it back. You already have `Relic.honed`,
`Relic.gemmed` and a reappraise button; this is mostly a re-shaping of what you have.

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. Keep the failure odds (they are the drama) but make failure **never destroy progress**
   — it costs drachma and the level stays. It already does this; say so loudly on the
   screen, because players assume the worst.
2. On every sub-stat roll at +3/+6/+9/+12, present **two candidates and let the player
   pick one**. This is the single change that converts rage into agency.
3. Add one stone tier that says "reroll this one sub stat, keep the other three."
4. Make the Halls of Essence and the Labyrinth pay these stones on a *schedule* the
   player can read, so a bad week has a floor.

### 4. A roguelike run mode built out of your existing battle engine
**Bucket A · about 3–4 weeks (the biggest item here)**

**What it is.** Honkai: Star Rail's **Simulated Universe** / **Divergent Universe**: you
clear randomly generated domains, pick up **Blessings** tied to Paths, and fight a boss
at the end of each World, turning "a mediocre team into a broken one for the length of a
run." It is the only source of Planar Ornaments, so every character without them is
"permanently missing half their gear." Arknights' **Integrated Strategies** is the same
shape — branching paths, **Collectibles** with upsides and downsides, and a per-run
**Command Level** that is separate from your account progression. Both are routinely
called the best-loved endgame in their games. [live]

**Why it fits Pantheon.** This is offline by nature — a seeded run against your own
engine — and it is the cheapest possible way to make your battle system feel new. You
already have the parts: `BattleEngine`, `StageBuilder` recipes for every realm,
`DungeonDatabase`, and a status system that can carry buffs. Your **100-floor Endless
Tower** is the natural host: keep the floors, add branching and blessings.

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. A run is 12–15 nodes on a branching map: battle, elite, shop, blessing, event, boss.
2. Write **40–60 blessings** grouped into five Paths (one per element) — "+30% crit
   damage", "your first skill each turn costs no cooldown", "burns spread on death".
   Numbers, not art. This is the whole content cost.
3. Buffs last the run only. Nothing persists but the rewards and a high-water mark.
4. Pay it in the relic stones from item 3 and in awakening essences, so it competes with
   the Halls for a player's time rather than duplicating them.
5. HSR's own 2026 quality-of-life fix is worth copying on day one: **save a cleared run's
   blessings as a file** so a player can re-challenge a specific boss without grinding
   the whole run again. [live]

### 5. A boss damage-check ladder where a unit may be used only once
**Bucket A · about 1–1.5 weeks**

**What it is.** AFK Journey's **Dream Realm**: four bosses on a daily rotation, five
attempts a day (ten if you invest), **six difficulty levels** where killing inside 90
seconds promotes you to the next, and a final **Endless** tier where the boss cannot die
and you are scored purely on damage dealt. Rewards scale with the **percentage of the
boss's HP you removed**, so a failed attempt still pays. Nikke's **Solo Raid** adds the
crucial constraint: an attempt is **five consecutive battles against one boss, and no
Nikke may be deployed twice**, so you need five distinct working squads. Blue Archive's
**Total Assault** scores you on speed of kill, and pays partial credit for HP removed
even on a loss. [live]

**Why it fits Pantheon.** You already have raids with barriers, adds and enrage. What you
do not have is the *scoring*, and scoring is what makes a boss worth fighting more than
once. The Nikke "no unit twice" rule is the best offline mechanic in this entire report:
it converts the depth of a player's box into a number, with **no leaderboard and no
server required**. A player with 15 built units beats a player with 6, and they can both
see exactly why.

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. One boss per pantheon, five bosses, one live each day of the week.
2. An attempt is three consecutive fights; a family used in fight 1 is locked out of 2
   and 3.
3. Score = total HP removed. Pay in bands (10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%, then every 25% past
   it), so there is always a next band visible.
4. Keep a **personal best** per boss and show "your best: 4.1M — next reward at 5M".
   A personal best is a leaderboard with one entry, and it needs no infrastructure.

### 6. Show the pity counter, and sell a unit the player chooses
**Bucket A · about 3–5 days — and part of it is legally required**

**What it is.** 2026 is being called the **"guaranteed-banner era"**: selector tickets,
sparks, guaranteed rate-ups and anniversary "pick any unit" tickets "have become standard
features in 2026, shifting from rare goodwill gestures to table stakes." Epic Seven gives
every new account a **Selective Summon** at creation and a **Moonlight Blessing** that
guarantees a Moonlight hero at set milestones, and its Mystic counter "carries over
between banners without expiration." Blue Archive runs a **mileage** system — one
Recruitment Point per pull, 200 points buys the featured character outright. Published
rates for reference: Genshin **0.6% base, hard pity 90, soft pity around 74, 50/50**;
Wuthering Waves **0.8%, hard pity 80**; Nikke **2% base, 200-pull hard pity**. [live]

**Why it fits Pantheon.** You have pity; the player cannot see it. An invisible pity
counter does none of the psychological work it was built to do. And — see the law section
— **Apple's Guideline 3.1.1 requires you to disclose the odds of each item type before
purchase**, so the odds screen is not optional the moment you ever sell a summon.

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. On the summon screen, under each banner: **"Guaranteed 5★ in 42 more summons"**, live,
   counting down.
2. A **Details** button on every banner showing the full rate table per grade and per
   unit. Write it now even though you sell nothing — it is a day's work and it is the
   compliance artefact.
3. Add a **mileage currency**: one point per summon on any banner, N points exchanges for
   any shipped family in that banner's pool. Your Night Market is the natural shop for it.
4. Give every new player a **selector** on their first day: pick one 4★ from a shortlist.
   Epic Seven's version of this is credited as one of its biggest F2P improvements. [live]

### 7. Progress that accrues while the app is closed
**Bucket A · about 4 days**

**What it is.** Nikke's **Outpost** generates resources passively while you are away.
Blue Archive's **Cafe** accrues AP and credits up to a cap, and comfort/furniture raises
the rate — at rank 5 and 3000 comfort it produces AP at about **1.63× the natural
recovery rate**, roughly 24 hours' worth. AFK Journey is built entirely on this: "your
team farms EXP, gold, and gear while the app is closed." Summoners War's **Scout Battle**
runs up to eight hours of offline farming. [live]

**Why it fits Pantheon.** You have a living island with the campaign team standing on it
and a wallet in the corner. Right now closing the app costs the player everything. An
accrual cap that fills in ~12 hours gives a reason to open the game twice a day that is
a *gift*, not a chore — and it is the opposite of the daily-checklist fatigue that the
2026 coverage identifies as the number one reason players quit. It is also trivially
offline: a timestamp, a rate, a cap, and a `Player` field (Optional, per your save rule).

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. `Player.lastIslandCollection: Date?` — Optional, per the save-field rule.
2. Rate = a function of highest chapter cleared; cap = 12 hours of it.
3. Pay drachma, unit EXP and a trickle of unknown scrolls. Never pay relics — the drip
   must not replace the farm.
4. Draw it as a pile of coin on the island that grows, with a tap-to-collect burst. The
   collection moment is the product.

### 8. A season track — the battle pass shape, with nothing to buy
**Bucket A · about 1 week**

**What it is.** Battle passes now appear in **nearly 60% of top-grossing mobile titles**
and account for **22% of all in-app purchase revenue globally**; where the industry IAP
conversion rate is under 2%, a battle pass sees "**70% of paying players buying the same
product**." One 2026 analysis puts the reason plainly: "the best thing the battle pass
system ever did was put a **ceiling** on spending." [live]

**Why it fits Pantheon.** Ignore the money. The battle-pass *shape* — a 30-day ladder
with a visible next reward, a points bar you fill by playing anything, and a hard end
date — is the strongest retention device free-to-play has produced, and it is pure
client-side bookkeeping. It also solves a problem you will hit: your daily missions,
feats and login gift are three separate reward lists that do not add up to a reason to
play on day 24. A season track collapses them into one bar.

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. A 30-day season keyed off `install date` (not a global clock — it is offline, and this
   way every player gets a full season).
2. Every activity in the game grants season points. Cap the daily points so a marathon
   session cannot finish it and a 20-minute day can keep pace.
3. 50 tiers. Show tier N+1 always. End the track with something loud — a selector ticket
   (item 6), a guaranteed 6★ Legend relic, an awakening cache.
4. **If he ever monetises**, this is the thing to sell — not summons. It has a price
   ceiling, it is certain rather than random, and it is the one purchase that does not
   attract the loot-box rules in the law section.

### 9. A clock-driven calendar so the game is different on Tuesday
**Bucket A · about 1 week**

**What it is.** Every live game in the genre runs a calendar; Summoners War's new
**Temple of Chaos** is "competitive PvE content… operating for **one week each month**,
starting on the first Monday," and its **Ameria's Luck** is a *daily* rotating drop-rate
buff. [live] The calendar is not the content — the calendar is the *reason to open the
app today rather than Saturday*.

**Why it fits Pantheon.** You cannot run real events without a server. You can run a
**perpetual calendar baked into the build**: the device clock tells you the day of week
and the week of year, and a table tells you what is different. Nothing phones home.

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. **Day of week:** one hall doubles its essence; one pantheon's banner gets a small rate
   bump (disclose it); one dungeon costs less energy.
2. **Week of month:** week 1 the Temple-of-Chaos-style gauntlet from item 1 rotates;
   week 3 a "double relic drop" week.
3. Put it on the island as a signboard: **"Today: the Hall of Tide pays double."**
4. Be honest in the store listing that this is a schedule, not live events. It is not a
   lesser thing; Another Eden built a following on exactly this posture.

### 10. The skip button, the auto button, and a story you can re-read
**Bucket A · about 1–2 weeks**

**What it is.** "Auto battle, dialogue skips, and clean quality of life features have
become standard, allowing players to enjoy good writing and tactical combat without a
huge daily commitment"; even Honkai: Star Rail shipped dialogue skip and auto-battle
retroactively. [live] The games with the longest tails are the ones read rather than
played: Fate/Grand Order retains **25% of new players past day 90** — extraordinary for
this genre — and takes **under 20% of its installs from paid ads**, which is a community
and a story, not a UA budget. [live] Reverse: 1999 passed **19.99M players** in August
2026 on the strength of its writing. [live]

**Why it fits Pantheon.** You have twelve chapters, five mythologies and a story line per
chapter shown on the map plate. That is currently a caption. Two things convert it into
retention: a **skip** that never punishes (so the player trusts you), and an **archive**
so the story can be read later, out of order, by someone who skipped it at 11pm.

**How to do it, concretely.**
1. Skip on every scene, always, with no reward difference. Auto-advance as the middle
   setting.
2. A **Chronicle** in the More menu: every chapter's text, re-readable, plus each
   family's lore (you already have lore behind the book on the unit sheet — surface it).
3. Give the boss lines you already have (`announceBoss`) a portrait and a name plate.
   You have 79 painted cards; a talking head costs nothing.

### Just outside the ten

- **iPad and Mac.** Cross-platform "influences approximately 41% of major new launches";
  Genshin, Wuthering Waves, Reverse: 1999 and Solo Leveling: Arise all run on PC and
  mobile with cross-save. [live] You cannot cheaply ship Steam from SceneKit/SwiftUI, but
  **iPad is nearly free and Apple silicon Macs run iPad apps**, and the game is landscape
  already. That is two of the three platforms for a week of layout work. **Bucket A.**
- **Epic Seven's Mystic Shop** — a rotating shop that sells the pull currency for a
  secondary currency. **You have already built this** (the Night Market, hourly refresh,
  paid re-roll). It is the right system and it is done; make sure summon currency is one
  of the things it can stock.
- **Guild / alliance.** The strongest social retention lever in the genre and the one
  thing genuinely worth a server later. **Bucket B.**

# 3. What the genre moved to since Summoners War (2014)

Pantheon inherits a 2014 design. Here is what 2025–2026 players treat as table stakes,
with a verdict on where Pantheon stands today.

The pattern is unmistakable: **almost every 2026 table stake is client-side.** The
genre's modernisation since 2014 has mostly been about *respecting the player's time and
reducing randomness* — both of which a solo dev with no server can do completely.

# 4. The honest list for a solo developer with no server

## Bucket A — works fully offline, single-player, no backend. Ship these.

1. **Sweep** (item 2).
2. **Open pity counter + published odds screen + mileage + first-day selector** (item 6).
3. **Choice-of-two on relic sub-stat rolls; a targeted reroll stone** (item 3).
4. **Idle accrual on the island with a 12-hour cap** (item 7).
5. **A season track keyed to install date** (item 8).
6. **A clock-driven daily/weekly modifier calendar** (item 9).
7. **A roster-breadth gauntlet with a season table** (item 1).
8. **A boss damage-check ladder with a no-repeat-unit rule and personal bests** (item 5).
9. **A roguelike run mode with blessings** (item 4).
10. **Dialogue skip + a Chronicle archive** (item 10).
11. **iPad and Apple-silicon Mac builds.**
12. **Personal bests and "your record" everywhere** — a leaderboard of one.
13. **A proper post-battle report** — damage by unit, turns, MVP. Free content from data
    you already compute, and it is what makes theorycrafting possible.
14. **Account-wide achievements with a visible completion percentage** (you have feats;
    show the percentage).

## Bucket B — needs a server or live-ops. Everything it implies.

| Thing | What it actually requires |
|---|---|
| **Real async PvP with other people's defence teams** | A player database, a matchmaking/rating service, defence-team snapshots, cheat validation (a client-authoritative result is free money for cheaters), and a seasonal reset job. Realistically a small backend, a database, and ongoing cost per DAU. |
| **Guilds** | All of the above plus chat with moderation, which is where your legal and support burden explodes (Brazil's ECA Digital requires **parental consent before enabling multiplayer communication** for minors — see the law section). |
| **Guild raids / co-op bosses** | Shared mutable state with concurrent writes. This is the hardest thing on the list and the one most likely to eat a solo dev alive. |
| **Real limited-time events with new units** | A content delivery pipeline, remote config, versioned asset bundles, and a release cadence you must then keep forever. The cadence is the commitment, not the tech. |
| **Leaderboards** | A server and anti-cheat. Note that Dream Realm, Total Assault and Solo Raid all work *mechanically* without the leaderboard — the ladder and the personal best carry most of the value. |
| **Real-money purchases** | Not a server, but StoreKit, receipt validation, restore, refunds, tax, and a compliance surface (see section 6). Meaningful work and meaningful ongoing obligation. |
| **Cross-save between devices** | An account system with all the identity and data-deletion duties that brings (including deletion on request). |
| **A/B testing and analytics-driven tuning** | An analytics pipeline plus a privacy policy and ATT prompts. |

## Bucket C — not worth copying, and why

1. **Simulated PvP where AI opponents are presented as real players.** Pantheon's arena
   is already AI-run and offline. **Keep it and label it honestly** ("Trial opponents",
   "Practice Arena"). Games that passed bots off as humans have drawn class actions and
   false-advertising claims — AviaGames was sued over allegedly "unbeatable" bots under
   California consumer law and federal racketeering law, and Skillz sued Papaya alleging
   it "falsely advertises that all players on its platform are human." [live] There is no
   upside to the lie and there is a real downside.
2. **Paid loot boxes / a real-money summon, at least at launch.** From **June 2026**, PEGI
   rates any newly submitted game with "paid random items" at **PEGI 16 minimum** [live];
   from **17 March 2026** Brazil's ECA Digital **bans paid loot boxes for minors** with
   penalties of **up to 10% of Brazilian revenue or R$50M per infraction** [live]; and the
   EU's Digital Fairness Act — proposal expected **Q4 2026** — may go as far as banning
   them, after an IMCO committee report passed **32–5–9** calling for exactly that [live].
   Selling *certainty* (a season pass, a character bundle, a cosmetic) attracts none of
   this. This is the single clearest strategic call in the report.
3. **FOMO-limited units that never return.** The 2026 coverage describes a genre "now
   competing on how much of that fear it can defuse." A solo dev cannot out-run a player
   base that has been trained to resent this, and it makes your back catalogue worthless.
4. **A daily checklist of eight-plus chores.** "The core issue with gacha dailies is that
   they intentionally replace actual gameplay with administrative chores, turning what
   starts as a fun hobby into a mandatory daily checklist that feels like an unpaid second
   job" [live]. You have eight daily missions. Cut them to **three, finishable in five
   minutes**, and move the rest to a weekly list.
5. **Energy as a wall.** Nikke's campaign costs no stamina at all; Limbus is designed so a
   busy adult can keep up [live]. In an offline game with nothing to sell, energy is a
   pure negative — it exists to sell refills, and you sell nothing.
6. **Chasing Genshin's production values.** Every open-world action gacha in the table
   costs tens of millions. Your competitive set is Epic Seven and Summoners War, and on
   *that* set your 3D battle with bespoke per-god animation is already competitive.
7. **A guild system built before you have players.** Empty guilds are worse than no
   guilds; this is the one Bucket B item that actively hurts at low scale.
8. **PC/Steam as a launch target.** Cross-platform is a real 2026 expectation, but the
   Steam 30% cut and a second engine target are not a solo dev's first month. iPad and
   Mac get you most of the perceived benefit for a week of work.

# 5. Retention and monetisation that is not predatory

## The benchmarks you are aiming at

Across all mobile games in 2026, medians have slipped to about **22% D1, 4% D7, under 1%
D30**; "good" is roughly **27% D1, 8–14% D7, 3–7% D30**. **RPGs have the highest D30
retention of any genre.** [live] For a ceiling: **Fate/Grand Order keeps 25% of new
players past day 90** [live] — that is the genre's outlier, and it is bought with a
decade of writing.

## What holds a player on day 7, day 30 and day 90 in a game like this

**Day 7 — "is there a next thing?"**
The player has cleared a few chapters and pulled a few units. What kills them here is a
*flat* day: same stages, same team, same drops.
1. The **season track** (item 8) gives an explicit next reward at all times.
2. The **daily/weekly modifier** (item 9) makes Tuesday different from Monday.
3. The **first-day selector** (item 6) means they have one unit they *chose*, which is the
   unit they build, which is the reason they come back.
4. Cut the daily list to three tasks.

**Day 30 — "does my box matter?"**
The player has 20–30 families and one good team. Everything else is dead weight, and that
is the moment a collector RPG either becomes a hobby or gets deleted.
1. The **roster-breadth gauntlet** (item 1) is the answer and there is no substitute.
2. The **no-unit-twice boss ladder** (item 5) is the second answer.
3. **Deterministic relic targeting** (item 3) is what stops the gear grind from reading as
   contempt at exactly this point in the curve.
4. **Sweep** (item 2) is what stops the farming from costing an hour a day.

**Day 90 — "am I still getting better?"**
1. The **roguelike** (item 4) — a run is different every time, so the same content is
   still content.
2. **Personal bests** — "beat your 4.1M" is an infinite goal and needs no server.
3. **A visible mastery surface** — achievement completion percentage, families at 6★,
   relics at +15, chapters at three stars on Hell. Collectors finish lists.
4. **The Chronicle** (item 10) — the one thing that makes a player talk about your game
   to someone else.

## If and when he sells something

He sells nothing today and may never. If he does, the order of safety is:

1. **A one-time "supporter" purchase** that pays a permanent small bonus (an extra sweep
   a day, a second island accrual slot, a cosmetic frame). No randomness, no ceiling
   problem, no loot-box law.
2. **The season pass** (item 8). Battle passes are bought by ~70% of paying players where
   the overall IAP conversion rate is under 2% [live], and they *cap* what a person can
   spend, which is the whole ethical argument in one sentence.
3. **Direct unit and relic bundles** — you know what you are buying.
4. **Cosmetics.**
5. **Paid summons — last, if ever.** They are the highest-revenue and by far the
   highest-risk item, and everything in section 6 attaches the moment you add one.

A premium up-front price is a harder road than it looks: free-to-play accounts for **96%
of mobile game downloads** [live], and premium mobile is "a niche thing," mostly ports and
cross-platform indies. A free game with a supporter purchase and a season pass is the
better shape.

# 6. Loot-box and gacha disclosure law, as of September 2026

**All [live] unless marked. I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice — but every
item below names a source he can hand to one.**

### What binds Pantheon the moment it charges money for a random pull

| Jurisdiction | Requirement | In force |
|---|---|---|
| **Apple App Store (worldwide)** | **Guideline 3.1.1**: apps offering loot boxes or other mechanisms providing randomised virtual items **for purchase** must **disclose the odds of receiving each type of item to customers before purchase**. | In force and enforced now |
| **China** | Ministry of Culture notice of December 2016: publish item contents and draw probabilities, and **keep draw records for at least 90 days**. | Effective 1 May 2017 |
| **South Korea** | Mandatory probability disclosure by law — **in the game, in advertising, and on the official website**. The Game Rating and Administration Committee opened a **Loot Box Victim Relief Center on 27 February 2026** and has said it will proactively investigate and mediate consumer-damage disputes. | Since **March 2024**; enforcement tightening through 2026 |
| **Japan** | **Kompu gacha** (complete-the-set gacha) ruled an illegal "card matching" premium scheme by the Consumer Affairs Agency on 18 May 2012. Ordinary gacha is legal, governed by **JOGA's 2012 guidelines** and **CESA's 2016 disclosure guidelines** (industry self-regulation, not statute). | Since 1 July 2012 |
| **Brazil** | **Lei 15.211/2025, the "ECA Digital"**, applies to any app "aimed at or likely to be accessed by" under-18s in Brazil: **paid loot boxes banned for minors**, 18+ content age-gated, maximum-protection defaults for minor accounts, **parental consent before enabling multiplayer communication**, no behavioural profiling of minors for ads, parental tools in Portuguese. Penalties **up to 10% of Brazilian revenue or R$50M per infraction**; both stores enforcing 18+ blocks at store level. | **Effective 17 March 2026** |
| **PEGI (Europe, ratings not law)** | Any game selling **"paid random items"** gets a **minimum PEGI 16** by default, some PEGI 18. Applies to **titles newly submitted from June 2026**; already-rated games are not re-rated unless they change. | **From June 2026** |
| **EU (proposed)** | The **Digital Fairness Act** is in the Commission's 2026 work programme for **Q4 2026** (draft expected Q3 2026). Under consideration: banning loot boxes outright or requiring parental consent for minors; **requiring the real-world money value of in-game items to be shown even when priced in virtual currency**. The European Parliament's IMCO committee adopted a report in late 2025 (**32 for, 5 against, 9 abstentions**) calling for the DFA to prohibit loot boxes, in-app currencies and pay-to-win in games likely accessed by minors. | **Not law yet** |
| **US** | No federal loot-box statute. Exposure is general **consumer protection / false advertising** (FTC Act and state equivalents) — which is what the bot-opponent cases above were brought under. | Ongoing |

### One more thing that binds him *today*, with no gacha at all

**Apple's age-rating overhaul.** Apple replaced 12+ and 17+ with new **13+, 16+ and 18+**
tiers, re-rated every existing app from prior questionnaire answers, and **required every
developer to complete the updated questionnaire — with new mandatory questions covering
in-app controls, app capabilities and violent themes — by 31 January 2026.** If Pantheon
has an App Store Connect record, that questionnaire is already overdue.

### What this means practically, in order

1. **Ship with no real-money summon.** Everything in the table above attaches to a *paid*
   random pull. A gacha paid for purely with earned in-game currency sits outside Apple
   3.1.1 and PEGI's "paid random items" rule, and outside Brazil's paid-loot-box ban.
   This is a real product advantage in 2026, and it is the position Pantheon is already in
   by accident. **Make it deliberate and say so on the store page.**
2. **Publish the odds anyway.** A rates table per banner and per grade is one screen and a
   day's work. It builds the trust that the "guaranteed-banner era" has taught players to
   expect, and it is the compliance artefact already built if he ever adds a paid summon.
3. **Show the pity counter.** Korea's law is about what the player can see; showing the
   counter is the same instinct, done for the player rather than the regulator.
4. **Complete Apple's new age-rating questionnaire now.** It is overdue and it gates
   updates.
5. **Assume a PEGI 16 the day a paid summon ships**, and decide whether that is acceptable
   before building the storefront, not after.
6. **Do not add chat.** Brazil's ECA Digital makes communication features a
   parental-consent obligation, and chat is a moderation duty a solo dev cannot discharge.
7. **Keep the arena honestly labelled.** AI opponents described as AI opponents cost
   nothing; AI opponents described as players are the AviaGames/Papaya fact pattern.
8. **If he ever adds real-money purchases, price in real money, not only in gems.** The
   EU's DFA is heading directly at virtual-currency obfuscation, and showing the real
   price is the cheap way to be ahead of it.

# 7. What I could not verify

Listed plainly. Do not treat any of these as facts.

1. **Every revenue figure in this report is a third-party estimate** relayed through a
   search engine's summary of a page I could not open. `eog.gg`, `hostedgg.com` and
   `revenue.ennead.cc` were all blocked by the egress proxy. Trackers disagree — I saw
   Zenless Zone Zero placed at **$37.8M (3rd) in July 2026** by one and **$9.66M (17th)
   in June** by another and **$11.8M in June** by a third. Directions are reliable;
   decimals are not.
2. **No September 2026 data exists yet.** August 2026 is the last complete month and the
   trackers publish 2–4 weeks in arrears.
3. **Summoners War's current monthly revenue.** One source states "no less than $20
   million per month for the past two years"; I could not establish the date of that
   statement or whether it still holds. Com2uS's Q1 2026 filing (revenue **down 13.9%
   YoY**, explicitly citing the SW off-season) points the other way.
4. **Epic Seven's global revenue and player count.** I found only **US-store** estimates
   (~6k downloads/$500k iOS, ~10k/$900k Google Play for its last reported month). Korea
   and Japan are where that game lives, and I have no figure for them.
5. **Path to Nowhere** — asked for by name, and I found **no** usable 2026 revenue or
   player data for it.
6. **Daily active user figures for the gacha titles.** The DAU data I found is for
   Roblox, Free Fire, Mobile Legends and Candy Crush. The one gacha figure — "Genshin
   Impact approximately 25–30 million global DAU" — comes from a marketing blog, is a
   suspiciously wide band, and I would not repeat it.
7. **RPG-specific D1/D7/D30 numbers.** Sources agree RPGs have the **highest D30 of any
   genre** but none of them printed the RPG row. The 22%/4%/<1% medians and the
   27%/8–14%/3–7% "good" band are all-genre figures.
8. **"Character-specific animated ultimates are a 2026 expectation"** — this is my own
   judgement from the fact that every game in the top table has them. I found no source
   that states it as a player expectation. Marked **[recall]**.
9. **"Guilds are near-universal in the genre"** — **[recall]**, not verified against a
   2026 source, and no source quantified their retention effect.
10. **Exact current gacha rates for Summoners War and Epic Seven.** I verified Genshin
    (0.6% / 90 / ~74 soft / 50-50), Wuthering Waves (0.8% / 80), Nikke (2% / 200 mileage)
    and Blue Archive (200 recruitment points). I did **not** verify SW's or E7's current
    published rates.
11. **The precise legislative status of the EU Digital Fairness Act.** Sources agree the
    proposal is scheduled for Q4 2026 with a draft in Q3 2026, but it is **not law**, its
    contents are not settled, and reporting on what it "might" ban is speculation.
12. **Another Eden Begins' commercial performance.** It is a strong precedent — a
    buy-to-play, gacha-free, offline single-player remake of a live gacha RPG, releasing
    on **17 September 2026** across Switch, Switch 2 and Steam — but it launches
    *tomorrow*, so there are no sales figures and there cannot be. Worth watching closely:
    it is the closest thing in the market to the shape Pantheon could take.
13. **Whether Pantheon's existing systems match what I have assumed.** I wrote this from
    the project brief and `CLAUDE.md`, not from reading the Swift. Where I say "you
    already have X", check it.
